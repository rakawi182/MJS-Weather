Methods and notes

C79 · two changes that alter values (all other C7x are display only). (1) ETA counted from the target time. The rain cells come from the hourly sum that ends at the next hour stamp, so they sit where the rain was on average, half an hour before that stamp. Until C78 the closest-pass time was counted from that position as if it were the target time, so within one hour the ETA hardly moved (in the logs of 21 and 23 Nov: 100.3 → 100.7 min after 30 min, 16.7 → 16.2 min after 30 min, 25.8 → 25.5 → 25.3 min after 46 min). Now the cell is moved on by the motion vector × (target time − centre of the field) before the pass is counted: t0 = t0(field position) − age. A cell whose closest point is already behind it drops out of the approach list (moving away or abeam), a cell in the next hour field is counted from its own centre. This applies to every motion vector, to the 300 members of the ETA cone (so ≤ 30 / ≤ 60 min and the P10–P90 move with it), to the flag inputs that use the ETA, to the ETA labels of the C73 rings, and to the C72 time lines of the rain map, which now sit at the distance of (minutes + age) × speed. Distances at the closest pass are unchanged. New column nb_field_age_min = minutes since the centre of the field (−30 … +30). The step at the hour boundary is not smoothed. The hourly sum is an average over a moving cell, so its centre is an approximation. [heur] (2) Radar fit not used at the search limit. If the best shift of the model field to the cold-cloud index lies on the border of the search window (±18 km), it is treated as no fit: the field is shown unshifted and rad_fit_ok = 0, rad_fit_dx_km = rad_fit_dy_km = 0; the best correlation is still logged. In the logs of 21 and 23 Nov about half of the fits sat on that border with correlations ≤ 0.6.

C78 · tidy-up of the rain map and one added number (display only; no value, flag, rule or CSV column changed). (1) The white outlines of the cold cloud after 30 and 60 min are drawn on a 3 km grid with gaps of one cell closed and regions below about 70 km² left out, and the 60 min outline is thinner and fainter; the cold-top squares are unchanged. (2) One line under the rain map explains the rain centroid cross, the pattern-shift arrow and the IR-drift arrow. (3) The pattern-shift arrow is thinner and fainter and is shortened when its tip would leave the map; its tooltip says that the direction often flips from one hour to the next. (4) The line “Cloud ≤ 235 K” of the arrival block shows the share of the cloud that cools ≥ 1.5 K per 10 min for the last scan pairs, for example 47 → 31 → 5 %; it is the same number as in the growth map, recomputed for earlier scan pairs, with no threshold or verdict, and it is not written to the CSV. A falling share means that the cloud is losing strength, which the edge ETA does not know (it assumes no growth or decay).

C73 · cell arrival marks on the “Rain, preceding hour” map (display only; no value, flag, rule or CSV column changed). The cells that the steering advection already evaluates in the arrival rows are marked on the map with the same rules: a cell with rain ≥ 0.5 mm/h is moved in a straight line with the steering wind; a solid ring with its ETA in minutes means the closest pass is ≤ 10 km and the model still has ≥ 0.5 mm/h at the arrival time; a dashed grey ring with “fades” means it passes ≤ 10 km but the model lets it die; a thin grey ring means the pass is 10–20 km away; a dotted grey ring means the closest point is already behind it (moving away or abeam). At most 8 labels are written (earliest first); every ring has a tooltip with the distance, rate now, closest pass and the model rate at arrival. Nothing is drawn when the steering wind is below 1 m/s or the flow is near-stationary. The ETA is the time of the closest pass, not the start of rain at the site, and only cells already raining are followed (new development is not covered). Together with the C72 time lines it shows which cell arrives when. [heur]

C72 · arrival time lines on the upwind wedge of every polar map in the Surroundings tab (display only; no value, flag, rule or CSV column changed). Dashed lines across the wedge at 30, 60 and 90 min: the line sits at distance steering speed × time upwind of the site, perpendicular to the steering direction, and is at most ±20 km wide (the approach limit). A rain cell on a line reaches the closest point of its straight-line pass in that many minutes, the same rule as the “steering advection” arrival rows, so the cells on each line show which one arrives when. Lines that fall outside the map radius are not drawn and the caption names them (at 10 m/s the 90 min line is 54 km out, beyond R = 36 km); lines closer than 12 % of R are left out, and none are drawn when the flow is weak or near-stationary (steering ≥ 1 m/s and not near-stationary are required). They follow the steering wind only, not the pattern shift, Corfidi vector or IR drift, which have their own arrows, and they ignore growth, decay and new development. [heur]

C71 · parallax, local flow and projected cold tops (display and CSV only; no flag, tier or fit uses them). (1) The “Rain, preceding hour” map of the Surroundings tab shows the cold tops of the latest IR scan under the dots (amber ≤ 235 K, orange ≤ 220 K, red ≤ 210 K). (4) Parallax: a high cloud top is seen displaced away from the sub-satellite point (Himawari-9, 0°, 140.7° E), so its true position lies toward it. The size is height × tan(satellite zenith angle) (≈ 34° at this site, ≈ 9 km for a 14 km top), the direction is the bearing to the sub-satellite point (≈ 76°, east-north-east); the height comes from the brightness temperature as (299 K − T) / 6.5 K/km, capped at 16.5 km (rule of thumb, about ±2 km, so about ±1.5 km of position). The tile offset of C18 (registration of the JMA tiles) is applied first and is a different thing; warm low cloud is shifted by less than 1.5 km and ≤ 235 K tops are the only ones drawn. (2) Local flow: the latest scan is compared with the scans 20–55 min earlier by block matching (zero-mean normalised cross-correlation on g′ = gray − 66, 9 × 9 px blocks every 4 px, ±6 px search, parabolic sub-pixel peak, correlation ≥ 0.5, at least 2 scan pairs that agree within 4 m/s, blocks that differ by more than 5 m/s from the median of their neighbours are dropped); blocks without a match are filled from valid blocks within 3 grid steps, else with the median. The grid reaches ±115 km. The cold-top pixels at their corrected positions are moved with this field in 5 min steps (no growth, decay or new development; the field is frozen at the scan time) and drawn as white outlines after 30 min (solid) and 60 min (dashed). The IR history card gets the share of a 10 km circle at the site covered by ≤ 235 K and ≤ 220 K cloud every 15 min up to 90 min after the scan, the range for flow speed ×0.7 to ×1.3 (a sensitivity, not a confidence interval) and the first time it reaches 50 %. CSV: fl_par_zenith_deg, fl_par_14km_km, fl_n_valid, fl_n_nodes, fl_spd_ms, fl_to_deg, fl_c30_pct, fl_c60_pct, fl_c90_pct, fl_c60_220_pct, fl_first_min (0 = already), fl_first_slow_min, fl_first_fast_min; compare with adv_c30_pct, adv_c60_pct and adv_first_min (one drift vector, no parallax). Limits: the scan time is that of the site line, other parts of the disc are scanned up to a few minutes earlier or later; the flow measures the motion of the cold-cloud pattern (anvil outflow included), not of the storm core; nothing is validated against rain yet. [heur]

C67 · B03 gray converted to albedo (rows x_vis_alb_*), empirical. HSD band 3 (0.5 km; albedo A = c′ I with c′ = 0.001928 and the calibrated slope and intercept of header block 5) was averaged to the 4.8 km tile grid and paired with the ALBD tile gray level over tiles 25/16 and 26/16 (101–124 °E, 0–10 °S, HSD segment 6) in three daytime scans on 22 Nov 2026 (00:30, 04:20 and 07:50 UTC; mean cos(solar zenith) 0.56, 0.96, 0.56). 225 071 pixels, tile-mean homogeneity limit 0.12, per-gray-level median made monotone. A is not divided by cos(solar zenith): that variant fitted worse (hold-out RMSE 0.085 against 0.034), so the tile follows A. Calibrated for gray 5–202 (albedo 0.03–0.70); outside it the value is clamped and x_vis_alb_over gives the share of pixels above the range, in which case the mean is a lower bound. Uncertainty: about ±0.005 at gray 64 (scene tables 0.120–0.130), ±0.03–0.05 at gray 128 (0.334–0.389), plus a systematic term of about −0.02 at gray 128–144 when the homogeneity limit goes from 0.04 to 0.12. The scatter of single 4.8 km pixels around the table is 0.01 at gray 16–32, 0.02 at 48–64 and 0.04–0.06 above gray 80, so one pixel is not better than that. Three scans of one day only; the sun-angle dependence is checked only within those scans. The Satellite IR card has a “B03 visible image (albedo)” switch: it shows the JMA B03 gray picture of the latest scan (daytime, same crop as B13) with the albedo scale bar, and a tap reads gray and albedo. Information only: no flag uses B03. [heur]

C65 · Himawari B03 (0.64 µm visible, JMA tile product ALBD) in the Satellite IR card, information only: nothing in the page uses it yet. For the latest scan and only in daylight (sun elevation ≥ 15°) the tiles covering the 20 km window are read at zoom 5 (4.8 km per pixel; zoom 6 and 7 returned no B03 tiles for this area on 22 Nov 2026), and the rows x_vis_* give mean, 5th and 95th percentile gray, texture (mean gray difference between neighbouring pixels), the share of pixels ≥ 128 and < 64, and the gray at the site pixel. The x_vis_mean to x_vis_site rows are the raw tile gray levels; C67 adds albedo rows from an empirical table (next note above). The aim is to see whether bright, bumpy tops (thick convective ice) can be told from smooth or dark cold cloud (thin cirrus, old anvil). At 4.8 km per pixel the window holds only about 4 pixels, so texture and the bright and dark shares are coarse. A missing B03 tile never affects the B13 or B08 results. [heur]

C38 · rain at the site versus rain nearby. Otherwise the card reads “No rain confirmed at the site; light rain in the model cell (~9 km)” with the nearest cell, and the interpretation panel no longer states rain at the site. Without a fresh site pixel the old rule stays, and the C32 light-rain branch (models disagree) is unchanged. Seen once (21 Nov 2026 09:31 UTC: IFS 0.3, median 0.5, ×1.5 → 0.45 mm/h, nearest cell 7 km NE, site pixel −19 °C, dry at the site); the thresholds are heuristics, not fitted. New CSV columns now_site_demoted, now_site_ir_k.

C37 · present weather rebuilt on the literature (WMO CIMO Guide ch. 14, WMO Cloud Atlas, Bennett & Vergari 2018, Zhang et al. 2021, Lee & Suh 2024, Lopez 2016). (1) Thunder: the cold-top limit is 218 K (−55 °C, was 213 K; CG lightning rises below ~218 K, any lightning below ~225 K); a cold core ≤ 218 K in the 20 km window, or ≤ 213 K in the ±28 km box while the cold shield grows, gives “thunder possible (distant cell, about 10 to 25 km)” because thunder is audible to about 20 km; the 10 km test stays for “probable”. The IR top is extrapolated for scan age by at most −3 K and only while the shield is not decaying. The CAPE ≥ 1000 gate (met in every tropical afternoon row) is now ≥ 800 J/kg and ≥ p50 of the local climatology. Your own thunder log keeps the label for 15 min after the last entry (the WMO past-weather idea: rain now, thunderstorm during the previous hour). (2) Rate: in a convective regime (ice top ≤ 235 K at the site, not decaying) the median of GFS, ICON and JMA may only raise the rate, never lower it. (3) Classes: WMO 2.5 / 10 / 50 mm/h instead of AMS 2.5 / 7.6; these are 3-min means, so the hourly-mean class is a lower bound for showers. (4) Showers = rain from a separate convective cloud, decided by the ice top at the site, not by the IFS showers share. (5) Drizzle only from stratiform low cloud (low cloud ≥ 50 %, no convective signal, CAPE < 500); otherwise “light rain shower” or “light rain”. (6) Icons: thunder without rain has no drops; “rain now, thunder in the hour before” shows a faded bolt. v8 · Himawari B13 grayscale (JMA tiles, z=5, ≈4.85 km/pixel). The 6 latest full-disk scans at or before the target time are read as JPEG tiles directly from jma.go.jp; the window is a 20 km circle around the site (two tiles when it crosses a tile edge; if a tile fails the scan is marked partial and gets no values). Values are grayscale levels (0–255) (brighter = colder) and, since v8.3, also brightness temperature in K from an empirical gray→K curve fitted to Himawari-9 HSD band 13 (6 scans, tiles 25/16 and 26/16, 3-parameter fit; leave-one-scene-out RMSE 0.87–1.08 K, bias within ±0.15 K, so the scale looked fixed across those scans). The curve is calibrated only for gray 23–247 (≈ 297 K to 193 K); outside that range it is extrapolated and marked. It is not an official JMA mapping, JPEG noise and the 4.85 km pixel smoothing still apply, and it was checked only on the two tiles named above. Each scan records the whole-tile minimum/maximum and the fraction of tile pixels at ≥ 254 or ≤ 1 (information only); the clipping warning applies only when pixels inside the 20 km window are at ≥ 254 or ≤ 1. This cannot prove the scale is not re-stretched. The image panel is a ±64 px crop of the tiles that were loaded (wide view = 3×3 tiles around the site tile, ±128 px crop, 9 requests per scan; missing extra tiles only leave black areas and never mark a scan partial); a chart below it shows window p95 and maximum gray level against time with the threshold as a dashed line; pixels ≥ the threshold are tinted warm; the 6 scans can be stepped or played, and the per-scan values of all 6 scans are written to the CSV. Mmodel field Aderived by the API Pcomputed in this page Finterpretive flag: rule applied to the fields above; the meter shows the share of criteria met (legend in the card); [heur] = heuristic threshold, not yet verified locally, [lit] = literature-based Xindependent source (Himawari, other models). Cloud layers: documentation is ambiguous (native vs humidity-based).

Daily probability: logistic regression P = 1/(1+exp(−(−6.88 + 10.16·x))), x = min–max normalised precipitable water (Sagita & Takemi 2025, Table 2; ERA5 2014–2022, thunderstorm days vs. no-significant-weather days; AUC 0.82; optimal threshold P = 0.48). Here it is applied to the daily mean IFS TCWV of one grid cell with a 2-year min–max (21.5–63.1 kg/m²); “alt-norm.” uses hourly extremes (18.5–71.3) and shows the sensitivity to that choice. It is a model score for a calendar day (WIB), not a verified local probability and not an hourly one. K-index and mid-level RH from the same paper need pressure levels, which the 9 km model does not provide.

CAPE × P is the physical product in W/m² (zero lag worked best in Romps et al. 2018). The flash-rate proxy uses the CONUS constant 1.3×10⁻¹¹ J⁻¹ (cloud-to-ground); the same paper shows the proxy fails to reproduce the land–ocean contrast, so treat it as relative intensity. √(2·CAPE) and √(2·CIN) are parcel-theory bounds without entrainment (ECMWF TM 852). IFS CAPE is not surface-based (see below); in Indonesia SBCAPE discriminated thunderstorm days poorly (AUC 0.69).

CAPE (ECMWF IFS): pseudo-adiabatic ascent, no entrainment; parcels from model levels up to 350 hPa, the lowest 60 hPa as 30 hPa mixed layers; the maximum is retained, so a surface-parcel CAPE is not provided (ECMWF, 47r3 CAPE/CIN notes). Which ECMWF field Open-Meteo maps to cape is not stated by Open-Meteo. CIN refers to the same most-unstable parcel since IFS 47r1.

Neighbourhood scan: one request with 69 locations (site + rings of 8, 16, 20 and 24 points at R/4, R/2, 3R/4 and R; C18: the point count follows the circumference and the 3R/4 ring closes the gap between 18 and 36 km) from the IFS 9 km run; nominal ring positions snap to the grid and duplicate cells are dropped. Upwind sector = cells within ±50° of the steering wind, the mean of the 850/700/500 hPa winds of the 0.25° open-data IFS (the 9 km run has no pressure levels); if that is unavailable the site 10 m wind is used and the label says so. The advection time is a straight-line estimate of cells with rain ≥ 0.5 mm/h moving with that wind; only cells whose closest point is still ahead count as approaching (a cell already within 20 km but moving away or abeam is counted separately); divergence is from the 10 m wind on each ring. All of it is diagnostic, not a forecast of initiation. v5 additions: cover and rain centroid are weighted by the area each cell represents (the rings are denser near the site); the pattern shift is the displacement of the rain centroid between the two latest hourly sums and mixes motion with growth and decay, and rain leaving the scan edge biases it; the Corfidi upshear vector (V_cloud-layer − V_850, with the 850/700/500 hPa mean standing in for the 850–300 hPa mean) was derived for organised convective systems and is only a hint of propagation elsewhere; when the steering wind is below 3 m/s, shear is ≥ 10 m/s or the levels disagree by ≥ 60° the straight-line advection is marked low confidence; the “model forecast” rows read the model’s own hourly rain at the scan cells and so include whatever motion and development IFS itself produces; the rain–elevation correlation is descriptive (a rank of terrain height only at 9 km resolution). v7: an advected cell counts as “on track” only if the model still has it raining (≥ 0.5 mm/h) at the arrival time, otherwise it is reported as fading; when it already rains at the site the approach criteria ask for renewed rain at +1 to +3 h and the flag says so. v6 adds the flags “rain cell approaching the site” (steering ETA, pattern-shift ETA, the model’s own forecast and upwind CAPE; the methods share the same IFS fields, so agreement between them is weaker evidence than it looks) and “rain tied to terrain”, a dashed 2 h pattern-shift arrow on the rain map, the model’s peak rain and 3 h site CAPE change, and an arrival table by method. All thresholds are heuristic and not verified against local observations. Slopes such as Penanggungan and Welirang remain unresolved at 9 km: the highest cell of the scan is listed.

Ensemble: IFS ENS 0.25° (~25 km cell, 51 members), raw member fractions without calibration, so a fraction describes the cell, not a point. CAPE per model: each model's CAPE is turned into a percentile inside its own local climatology (same 6 h block); press Calibrate cell once to download the GFS and ICON climatologies.

With a complete B13 scan it is fitted to the satellite: the field is moved by the shift (up to 18 km, 3 km steps) that best correlates log(rain + 0.1) with the cold-cloud index, accepted only when the correlation reaches 0.3 and gains at least 0.05, and at “now” the rain is multiplied by a gate that is 1 at ≤ 235 K and 0 at ≥ 265 K. The map is a smoothed model field, not a radar: it cannot show cells smaller than the 9 km grid, the shift is a displacement guess that is applied to the forecast frames too, and the IR scan lags the target time by the age shown. All thresholds are heuristic and unverified; the shift and the correlations are written to the CSV (rad_*).

C17 · pixel export: the satellite tab has a button that saves the 19 × 19 gray block (≈ ±44 km) of every IR scan seen in the session as ir_tile_pixels.json. Together with b13_box_pixels.npz from himawari_b13_site.py, b13_compare_tiles.py compares tile and HSD pixel by pixel, tests a geolocation shift and refits the gray → K curve.

C9 · stability indices: K-index, Total Totals, Vertical/Cross Totals, Showalter and Lifted Index are computed in this page from T and RH at 850/700/500 hPa of the open-data IFS 0.25° (same request as the steering wind; Td from T and RH over water). Lifted Index uses the surface parcel at the target height, Showalter the 850 hPa parcel; both follow a pseudo-adiabat (θe, Bolton 1980) to 500 hPa and are not the same as the IFS CAPE, whose parcel is the most unstable one below 350 hPa. The 10 m → 500 hPa shear approximates 0–6 km shear. These are descriptive values at 0.25° (~25 km); no flag is built on them because published thresholds come mostly from mid-latitudes and have not been checked against local outcomes.

C10 · steering and IR motion: the steering wind is now the pressure-weighted (trapezoid in p) mean of the 925, 850, 700, 600 and 500 hPa winds of the 0.25° open-data IFS, a cloud-layer mean instead of the 850/700/500 average; shear and the direction spread are still taken from 850/700/500 hPa. The winds at 400, 300, 250 and 200 hPa come with the same request and are used only to identify what the IR cloud motion follows: the vector difference to the model wind is computed at every level, and when the best level lies above 400 hPa (and the difference is ≤ max(2 m/s, 30 % of the IR speed)) the vector is shown as high-cloud drift. It is then left out of the rain arrival times, the “methods in ≤ 3 h” count and the approach flag, but the time the cloud pattern would reach the site is still listed. When the mean wind is below 3 m/s and the shear below 10 m/s the flow is labelled near-stationary and propagation-dominated, and the arrival rows give a range over the methods instead of one time. The 3 m/s, 10 m/s and 30 % limits are heuristic and unverified. Tracking the coldest cell cores and checking the arrival times against observed rain onset are not part of this version.

C12 · IR flags: the third criterion of “Cold-topped cloud near site” is now met by a rising p95 (≥ 15 levels per 10 min) or by a cold top that has persisted (p95 at or above the threshold in every scan of the last 30 min, at least 3 scans over at least 20 min), so a mature storm can reach L3 instead of stopping at L1; it can no longer tell a mature core from a lingering anvil. “Rapidly cooling cloud tops” is unchanged and stays a growth sign only, so it reads L0 on a mature storm. The CSV gets x_ir_mot_n and x_ir_mot_N, the scan pairs used and tried for the IR cloud motion.

C10 · cloud-top cooling: for every pixel the brightness temperature of the latest B13 scan is compared with the scan about 20 min earlier (10 min if none), sampled at the position the cloud had then (the IR cloud motion removed, bilinear) and expressed per 10 min after a 3 × 3 mean. A pixel counts as rapidly cooling when it cools by 3 K per 10 min or more, is colder than 260 K and has at least 3 more such pixels among its 8 neighbours; pixels in the saturated cold range (gray above 247) are left out. Tick “cooling” under the satellite image to overlay it on the latest scan (magenta = rapid, violet = cooling at half that rate). One motion vector is used for the whole window, so wind shear, parallax and cloud-edge gradients can leave false cooling or warming along edges, and the thresholds are heuristic and not checked against observed rain. The flag “rapidly cooling cloud tops” only describes growth in the IR; it does not say where or when rain will fall.

C9 · IR cloud motion: in a 41 × 41 px window (≈ 200 km) around the site, the pattern of clouds colder than ≈ 280 K (gray above 66) is matched between pairs of B13 scans 20–60 min apart (zero-mean normalised cross-correlation, ±6 px search, parabolic sub-pixel peak); the median velocity of all pairs with correlation ≥ 0.6 is used, and at least 3 pairs are required. It measures how the cold-cloud pattern drifts, which includes anvil and cirrus outflow, growth and decay, so it can differ from the steering wind and from the motion of single cells. Pixels are ≈ 4.85 km and parallax is not corrected. The window moves off the site when the site is near the edge of the loaded tiles (offset shown). It is the fifth arrival method and one more criterion of the approach flag.

v4: charts, rule-based summary and terrain flags are computed in this page from the same IFS fields (P / [heur], not verified locally). Weather code (Open-Meteo) is derived from cloud cover, precipitation, snowfall and CAPE (HRES 9 km also showers, CIN and boundary-layer height; thresholds not documented) and is an instantaneous value, while precipitation is the preceding-hour sum: compare the code with the precipitation of the hour before its timestamp, not with the hour-end value shown above. API responses are cached in memory for 10 min and retried on network errors, HTTP 429 and 5xx.
